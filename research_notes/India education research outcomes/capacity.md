# Capacity link in India's education and R&D chain: researchers, institutions and higher-education capacity

Researcher: subagent notes for the India-first macro report. All live facts were retrieved on **access date 2026-11-20** (today); each bullet gives the underlying observation year/date. No memory-based facts are used in any headline claim. Sources are free/public only; paid sources appear only in the final "later-report gaps" note. ## Key question 1: Researchers and technicians in R&D — India's level, trend, peers, and how good the series is

### Takeaway
India's official DST/NSTMIS stock of full-time-equivalent (FTE) researchers doubled in a decade but remains among the thinnest in the comparison set: **5.07 lakh FTE researchers as of 1 April 2024 (354 per million)** versus roughly 2,100–9,500 per million in China and Korea. The internationally comparable World Bank/UNESCO series is **extremely sparse for India (6 observations, 2000–2020, for researchers; 5 observations, 2000–2018, for technicians)** and should be shown with its observation years, not interpolated.

Only 6 non-null observations in 2000–2025; no value for 2021–2025. — [World Bank API, SP.POP.SCIE.RD.P6, accessed 2026-11-20](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA/indicator/SP.POP.SCIE.RD.P6?format=json&per_page=1000&date=2000:2025)

- **CONFLICT — researcher levels differ by source and year label:** World Bank/UNESCO India **2020: 259.263**; DST says **262 in 2020-21**, **354 in 2023-24**; DST's Table 24 labels 354 as "2023" while the At-a-Glance narrative labels it "2023-24". ### Inferences
- India's researcher stock roughly tripled per million between 2000 and 2023-24 by its own source (110.6 in 2000 on the UNESCO series → 354 in 2023-24 on DST's series), but the *gap* to Korea and Germany did not close: at DST's 2023 comparison, Korea was ~27× and Germany ~17× India.
- Within India's R&D workforce, higher education is the single largest employer of FTE R&D staff (238,728 of 507,351, ~47%) — so faculty capacity in universities is directly R&D capacity, not just teaching capacity. (Computed from DST Table 21, accessed 2026-11-20.)
- The World Bank series' "latest year" column is misleading for peers: Brazil's latest researcher observation is 2014 and the UK's is 2017. Any peer bar chart must print the year on each bar.

### Gaps
- No India technician headcount in DST NSTMIS's current *At a Glance* or *S&T Indicators Tables* 2025-26; the only public source found is the UNESCO/WB series ending 2018. Search for an older DST "R&D Statistics" edition with a human-resources-by-category table before making any technician claim.
- No machine-readable NSTMIS API found — publications are PDFs; the digital repository is a legacy PHP site. A dated snapshot CSV would be needed for the library.
- No free, annual, harmonised researcher count for India after 2023-24 as of 2026-11-20.

## Key question 2: Higher-education capacity — institutions, enrolment, gross enrolment ratio and faculty

### Takeaway
The latest AISHE final report (2023-24) records **4.50 crore students in 59,553 responding HEIs (1,278 universities, 46,468 colleges, 11,787 standalone institutions)** with **17.3 lakh teachers** and a GER of **30.0** on a 2011-census population projection. World Bank/UNESCO's tertiary GER is higher (India **34.45 in 2025**) because it uses a different enrolment source and population denominator — a definite conflict to disclose, not average.

### Cited Findings
- **Stock — AISHE 2023-24 (latest final report available as of access date):** frame of 64,756 HEIs; 59,553 participated (~92% response); after pooling 2021-22 and 2022-23 records, **1,278 universities, 46,468 colleges, 11,787 standalone institutions**. Actual 2023-24 responses were lower (1,249 universities / 44,380 colleges / 10,513 standalone), so published totals include pooled earlier-year data. — [AISHE, *AISHE Final Report 2023-24*, Box 1 p.13 and Executive Summary p.4, accessed 2026-11-20](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2026/07/202607131602421770.pdf) (report index: [aishe.gov.in/aishe-final-report](https://aishe.gov.in/aishe-final-report/))
- **Stock/flow — enrolment (AISHE 2023-24):** total enrolment **4.50 crore (45,00,01,123)**, up from 4.46 crore in 2022-23 and 3.85 crore in 2019-20; UG 76.8%, PG 12.9%, diploma/certificate 7.4%; women 49.7% of enrolment. — [AISHE Final Report 2023-24, pp.1–4 and Figure 60, accessed 2026-11-20](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2026/07/202607131602421770.pdf)
- **Ratio — AISHE GER, 18–23 years:** **30.0 in 2023-24** (male 28.9, female 31.2, GPI 1.08), up from 29.5 in 2022-23 and 23.7 in 2014-15. **Method note from the report:** "GER for AISHE 23-24 has been calculated on Population Projection derived from 2011 Census." — [AISHE Final Report 2023-24, pp.2, 5, 35, accessed 2026-11-20](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2026/07/202607131602421770.pdf)
- **CONFLICT — World Bank/UNESCO tertiary GER (same concept, different numbers):** India **2025: 34.4465**, 2024: 34.4185, 2023: 34.2394, 2021: 31.5742 (annual series, 26 non-null points 2000–2025). Metadata: UIS Data API, date accessed **2026-03-19**, published 2026-02; GER = enrolment regardless of age ÷ official school-age population. AISHE's 30.0 for 2023-24 vs UIS ~34.2 for 2023 is a ~4-point gap — use one source per chart and cite the denominator. — [World Bank API, SE.TER.ENRR, accessed 2026-11-20](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA/indicator/SE.TER.ENRR?format=json&per_page=1000&date=2000:2025); [World Bank indicator metadata, accessed 2026-11-20](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/SE.TER.ENRR?format=json)
- **Ratio — peer tertiary GER, latest available (World Bank/UIS, each with its own year):** Korea **2024: 111.853**, UK **2023: 80.415**, Germany **2024: 76.710**, China **2024: 76.876**, Brazil **2024: 69.732**, USA **2022: 79.362**, India **2025: 34.447**. — [World Bank API, SE.TER.ENRR, accessed 2026-11-20](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA/indicator/SE.TER.ENRR?format=json&per_page=1000&date=2000:2025)
- **Stock — faculty (AISHE 2023-24):** **17.3 lakh teachers (+0.7 lakh over 2022-23)**, 55.1% male / 44.9% female; **PTR 22 (regular mode)** in the Executive Summary, but the Key Results bullet says **PTR 23 for universities and colleges, regular mode**, and 19 for universities and constituent units — an internal inconsistency within the same report. — [AISHE Final Report 2023-24, pp.2–4, accessed 2026-11-20](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2026/07/202607131602421770.pdf)
- **Stock — UGC's university count (as on 31 March 2024):** **57 central, 482 state public, 478 state private, 136 deemed universities** (total 1,153). This is a legal/list-based UGC count, not AISHE's 1,278 participating "universities/university-level institutions"; do not treat the two as the same universe. — [UGC, *Annual Report 2023-24*, Chapter 2.2.1, p.109, accessed 2026-11-20](https://www.ugc.gov.in/pdfnews/UGC_Annual_Report_2023_24_English.pdf)
- **Lag/definition — UGC GER:** UGC's 2023-24 annual report publishes GER only to **2021-22: 28.4%** (male 28.3, female 28.5) and explicitly says "GER data for the years 2022-23 and 2023-24 is currently unavailable". Its table is sourced from AISHE reports; AISHE's later time series puts 2021-22 at 28.4 too, so this is a publication lag, not a contradictory estimate. — [UGC Annual Report 2023-24, Chapter 2.1, p.108, accessed 2026-11-20](https://www.ugc.gov.in/pdfnews/UGC_Annual_Report_2023_24_English.pdf)
- **Definition caution — AISHE is a voluntary survey:** "responsibility for the accuracy of the data rests with the Nodal Officers of the concerned HEIs"; DoHE only does inbuilt validation/scrutiny. Coverage depends on institutions uploading data. — [AISHE Final Report 2023-24, "Information to our Readers", p.v, accessed 2026-11-20](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2026/07/202607131602421770.pdf)

### Inferences
- India's higher-education system is large in headcount but still low in participation relative to peers: 34.4% tertiary GER versus 70–112% in China, Korea, Germany, UK, USA and Brazil (World Bank/UIS, latest years 2022–2025; accessed 2026-11-20).
- Both "GER" numbers are defensible but answer different questions: AISHE's uses a 2011-census-based denominator and AISHE enrolment; UIS/WB uses its own enrolment and UN population. A student-facing chart should show one series with a note, not blend them. (Inference from the two cited sources.)
- Institution counts grew faster than the UGC's legal list implies because AISHE's "university/university-level institutions" category and pooled-response method are broader. (Inference from AISHE Box 1 and UGC Chapter 2.2.1.)

### Gaps
- AISHE 2024-25 final report was **not published** on the AISHE final-reports page as of 2026-11-20 (latest listed: 2023-24); the portal advertises registration for 2025-26/2026-27, not final data.
- No free UIS API key was used; all UIS values here come via the World Bank API (which supplies its own access date). A direct UIS pull would need a free API token and was not attempted.
- No consistent time series of *number of universities/colleges* was extracted from AISHE beyond the 2023-24 snapshot and the five-year totals embedded in figures; use AISHE's Excel tables if a series is needed.

## Key question 3: PhD and STEM graduates — the flow that feeds the researcher stock

### Takeaway
AISHE 2023-24 shows a large and fast-growing PhD **enrolment stock** (**3,43,559**) but a much smaller **degree flow** (**37,601 PhD pass-outs** based on actual responses); STEM enrolment was **1.01 crore** and B.E./B.Tech out-turn **8.2 lakh**. The same AISHE report gives a second, conflicting PhD enrolment figure (**2,92,552**) — use the number with its definition attached.

### Cited Findings
- **Flow/stock — PhD enrolment (AISHE 2023-24):** main text: "The total enrolment for Ph.D. Programme is **3,43,559**"; Science 23.4% (~80,284 students), Engineering & Technology 20.5%. Trend: "Total Ph.D. enrolment has increased from **1.17 lakhs in 2014-15 to 3.44 lakhs in 2023-24**"; female PhD enrolment from 47,717 to 1.71 lakh. — [AISHE Final Report 2023-24, pp.27–28, 56, accessed 2026-11-20](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2026/07/202607131602421770.pdf)
- **CONFLICT — same report, different PhD enrolment:** Box 13's five-year table gives **2023-24: 2,92,552** (male 1,50,461, female 1,42,091), 2022-23: 1,99,218, 2021-22: 1,84,376 — versus 3,43,559 in the narrative and Box 6 (Ph.D. in regular mode 2,92,552). The apparent 47% jump from 2022-23 to 2023-24 in Box 13 is likely a reporting/definition change; **do not plot these as a clean series without checking AISHE's estimation method**. — [AISHE Final Report 2023-24, Box 6 p.36, Box 13 p.57, accessed 2026-11-20](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2026/07/202607131602421770.pdf)
- **Flow — PhD degrees/awards (AISHE 2023-24, actual response only):** Table 37 "Grand Total" for Ph.D. out-turn = **37,601** (male 19,860, female 17,741); discipline leaders: **Science 8,211**, **Engineering & Technology 7,028**. AISHE defines PhD out-turn as "the number of students, who are awarded degree during two convocations falling prior to the reference date" — so it is not a cohort completion rate. — [AISHE Final Report 2023-24, pp.43, 212 and Annexure definitions, accessed 2026-11-20](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2026/07/202607131602421770.pdf)
- **Stock/flow — STEM (AISHE 2023-24):** enrolment in Science (incl. Mathematics) + Engineering & Technology across UG/PG/M.Phil./Integrated/Ph.D. = **1,01,88,988 (10.19 million; 22.5% of total enrolment, actual response)** — 57,06,160 male, 44,82,828 female. Engineering & Technology enrolment 46,33,868; Science 55,55,120 (of which PhD Science 80,284). — [AISHE Final Report 2023-24, pp.3, 28–29, accessed 2026-11-20](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2026/07/202607131602421770.pdf)
- **Flow — undergraduate STEM-style degrees (AISHE 2023-24 out-turn):** B.E./B.Tech combined **8.2 lakh**; B.Sc **11.51 lakh**; BCA and B.Pharm are the fastest-growing UG programmes over 2019-20 to 2023-24 (Box 14). Total out-turn ~1.1 crore. — [AISHE Final Report 2023-24, pp.3–4, 58, accessed 2026-11-20](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2026/07/202607131602421770.pdf)
- **Flow/attrition context — named press report:** *The Hindu* (published 2025-04-15, updated 2025-04-24) cited **AISHE 2021-22: >42,000 science PhD registrations and ~7,200 science PhD degrees** and said "some estimates put drop-out figures at as high as 40%". The 40% is **unnamed in the article** — do not use as a headline statistic. — [Rohini Karandikar, *The Hindu*, "Doctoral distress: What's broken in India's PhD system and how to fix it", accessed 2026-11-20](https://www.thehindu.com/education/doctoral-distress-whats-broken-in-indias-phd-system-and-how-to-fix-it/article69453559.ece)
- **Stock — field of specialisation (DST/NSTMIS, 01.04.2024):** engineering/technology 1,41,144 full-time personnel, medical 61,910, natural sciences 32,528, agricultural 27,269, social sciences 5,772; **explicitly excludes Higher Education** because field data are not available there. — [DST NSTMIS, *S&T Indicators Tables 2025-26*, Table 23, accessed 2026-11-20](https://www.nstmis-dst.org/Pdfs/ST%20INDICATORS%20TABLES%202025-26.pdf)

### Inferences
- The PhD pipeline is expanding strongly on the enrolment side (1.17 lakh → 3.44 lakh in nine years) while awards remain ~37,600/year on an actual-response basis; a raw awards/enrolment ratio (~11%) is **not** a completion rate because stocks, flows, response bases and convocation windows differ. (Computed from AISHE figures cited above.)
- STEM enrolment (22.5%) and engineering out-turn (8.2 lakh) are large, but the research-relevant flow is tiny by comparison: ~7,000 engineering PhD degrees/year. (AISHE 2023-24, accessed 2026-11-20.)

### Gaps
- No official India time series of PhD *completions* or cohort completion/attrition rates was found; AISHE publishes awards in a convocation window, not a cohort follow-up.
- No free, harmonised cross-country STEM-graduate or doctoral-graduate flow dataset was retrieved for the peers. World Bank/UIS tertiary GER is a participation ratio, not graduate counts. This is a real comparator gap.
- The Kannan et al. (2024) IGI Global chapter "Attrition Among Indian Social Science Doctoral Scholars: An Exploration of the Contributing Reasons" exists and reportedly uses ~200 participants, but the publisher page returned 429 and ResearchGate returned 403 on 2026-11-20 — findings were **not verified** and are excluded from claims. — [IGI Global listing](https://www.igi-global.com/viewtitle.aspx?TitleId=363205&isxn=9798369360408) (access attempted 2026-11-20)

## Key question 4: School-level capacity as context (brief)

### Takeaway
UDISE+ 2024-25 is the latest school census: **14.71 lakh schools, 1.01 crore teachers, 24.69 crore students**, with pupil–teacher ratios of **20 (primary), 17 (upper primary), 15 (secondary), 23 (higher secondary)**. Its own caveat: from 2022-23 data are student-wise and **not strictly comparable with 2021-22 or earlier**.

### Cited Findings
- **Stock — UDISE+ 2024-25 (latest report):** "nearly **14.71 lakh schools, more than 1.01 Cr teachers and nearly 24.69 Crore students** from pre-primary to higher secondary". Pre-primary enrolment excludes Anganwadi ECCE centres and private kindergartens. — [Department of School Education & Literacy, *UDISE+ 2024-25 Booklet*, Key Results p.10, accessed 2026-11-20](https://dashboard.udiseplus.gov.in/report2026/static/media/UDISE+2024_25_Booklet_existing.118ba29d4773e6372f72.pdf)
- **Ratio — PTR, India 2024-25 (Table 4.12):** primary (1–5) **20**, upper primary (6–8) **17**, secondary (9–10) **15**, higher secondary (11–12) **23**. — [UDISE+ 2024-25 Booklet, pp.9, 63, accessed 2026-11-20](https://dashboard.udiseplus.gov.in/report2026/static/media/UDISE+2024_25_Booklet_existing.118ba29d4773e6372f72.pdf)
- **Flow/ratio — dropout and retention 2024-25:** dropout primary **0.3%**, upper primary **3.5%**, secondary **11.5%**; retention class 1→12 **47.2%**; transition rates primary→upper primary 92.2, upper primary→secondary 86.6, secondary→higher secondary 75.1. — [UDISE+ 2024-25 Booklet, Key Results p.62, accessed 2026-11-20](https://dashboard.udiseplus.gov.in/report2026/static/media/UDISE+2024_25_Booklet_existing.118ba29d4773e6372f72.pdf)
- **Infrastructure 2024-25:** **93.6%** of schools have electricity connection, **99.3%** drinking water facility. — [UDISE+ 2024-25 Booklet, Key Results p.62, accessed 2026-11-20](https://dashboard.udiseplus.gov.in/report2026/static/media/UDISE+2024_25_Booklet_existing.118ba29d4773e6372f72.pdf)
- **Definition break (important):** "UDISE+ 2024-25 data is not strictly comparable with the previous reports upto 2021-22 on various educational indicators like GER, NER, Dropout rates etc. However, **2022-23, 2023-24 & 2024-25 are comparable**." Student-wise data collection began in 2022-23; West Bengal supplies bulk data from its own MIS rather than feeding the portal directly. — [UDISE+ 2024-25 Booklet, Key Results pp.10–11, accessed 2026-11-20](https://dashboard.udiseplus.gov.in/report2026/static/media/UDISE+2024_25_Booklet_existing.118ba29d4773e6372f72.pdf)
- **CONFLICT/definition — PTR naming:** a PIB release (search-result snippet, original page returned 403 to fetch on 2026-11-20) reports PTR **10 (Foundational), 12 (Preparatory), 17 (Middle), 21 (Secondary)** and teacher counts **94,83,294 (2022-23), 98,07,600 (2023-24), 1,01,22,420 (2024-25)**; these use NEP stage groupings and are not the same levels as the booklet's primary/upper-primary/secondary/HSE groupings. Treat as complementary, not contradictory, but verify the PIB page before quoting it. — [PIB press release, PRID 2161543, snippet via web search, accessed 2026-11-20](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2161543)

### Inferences
- School capacity is near-universal in enrolment and infrastructure inputs; the visible weaknesses in the official data are upper-secondary retention/dropout, not access. (Inference from UDISE+ 2024-25 figures cited above.) Learning outcomes are covered by the outcomes researcher and are deliberately not repeated here.
- The 2022-23 methodology change means any "school capacity trend" must start in 2022-23. (Inference from the report's own comparability caveat.)

### Gaps

## Key question 5: Attributable evidence on research-capacity constraints in India

### Takeaway
The strongest free, attributable evidence is the **364th Report of the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education (March 2025)**: as on 31 December 2024 / 31 January 2025, **28.56% of 18,940 sanctioned teaching posts in central higher-education institutions were vacant**, rising to **56.18% of professorships**, and the committee flags lab input costs. A separate DST evaluation documents what instrument access has delivered; no verified India-specific early-career-funding constraint study was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Constraint — faculty vacancies (named primary report):** 364th Report, Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports, *Demands for Grants 2025-26 of the Department of Higher Education* (presented March 2025). As on 31.12.2024: central universities **18,940 sanctioned teaching posts, 13,530 filled, 5,410 vacant (28.56%)**; CFTIs 1,388 sanctioned, 374 vacant (26.94%). As on 31.01.2025, UGC-funded central universities: **Professor 2,540 sanctioned / 1,113 filled / 1,427 vacant (56.18%)**; Associate Professor 38.28%; Assistant Professor 17.97%. Committee: vacancies "impact the faculty-student ratio and dilutes the quality of teaching"; recommends phasing out excessive contractual employment. — [Rajya Sabha Secretariat, 364th Report, §§3.14.1, 3.14.8, 3.14.14, accessed 2026-11-20](https://sansad.in/getFile/rsnew/Committee_site/Committee_File/ReportFile/16/198/364_2025_3_15.pdf?source=rajyasabha)
- **Constraint — reserved-category pipeline:** same report, observation 56: difficulty filling reserved faculty posts (e.g. ST (Hills) in Assam) is attributed to lack of a sustained school-to-PhD pipeline, and the committee notes the **discontinuation of the National Level Science Talent Search Examination (NSTSE)** as a blow to that pipeline; recommends exploring its restart with NCERT and DoSE&L. — [364th Report, observation 56, accessed 2026-11-20](https://sansad.in/getFile/rsnew/Committee_site/Committee_File/ReportFile/16/198/364_2025_3_15.pdf?source=rajyasabha)
- **Constraint — lab/instrument costs (committee observation, not independently audited):** same report §3.13.17 notes **18% GST on construction of buildings and GST on equipment and chemicals for laboratory research**, and says "the cost of equipment for scientific research and laboratories has increased many folds due to the increase in custom duties on lab chemicals from **10% to 150%**"; recommends tax rebates for HEIs. — [364th Report, §3.13.17, accessed 2026-11-20](https://sansad.in/getFile/rsnew/Committee_site/Committee_File/ReportFile/16/198/364_2025_3_15.pdf?source=rajyasabha)
- **Corroboration of the vacancy finding (secondary):** *University World News*, 3 April 2025, reported the same 28.56% overall / 56.18% professorial vacancy rates from the committee report, and quoted Prof. Aarti Srivastava (NIEPA) that students prefer quick-return fields to costly, time-intensive PhDs, leaving faculty posts short of qualified candidates. — [Shuriah Niazi, *University World News*, accessed 2026-11-20](https://www.universityworldnews.com/post.php?story=20250403141956878)
- **Lab/instrument capacity — what funded access achieved (named evaluation):** DST/NSTMIS *Impact Evaluation Report: Funds for Improvement of Science & Technology Infrastructure (FIST) Scheme* (Jan 2021, executive summary). In southern India, **581 FIST projects sanctioned 2000–2011 for ₹41,318 lakh** (42.95% of national sanctioned amount); a survey found **2,620 equipment items procured and used by internal and external users**, plus internet, IPR cells, incubation centres and computation facilities, with reported improvements in publications, patents and collaborations. This is a positive evaluation of a targeted intervention, not a measured national shortfall. — [NSTMIS, FIST impact evaluation executive summary, accessed 2026-11-20](http://digitalrepository-nstmis-dst.org/pcr/es/308/308/Executive-Summary.pdf) (DST announcement: [dst.gov.in/news/impact-evaluation-report-fist-scheme](https://dst.gov.in/news/impact-evaluation-report-fist-scheme))
- **Early-career support — current scale (policy response, not evidence of a shortfall):** ANRF's Prime Minister's Early Career Research Grant offers up to **₹60 lakh plus overheads over three years, maximum 700 grants per year across all subjects**, and subsumes the erstwhile SERB Startup Research Grant. — [ANRF, PM ECRG guidelines, accessed 2026-11-20](https://anrfonline.in/ANRF/ecrg_anrf)
- **Early-career/extramural context (flow):** extramural R&D project support was **₹3,356.36 crore across 6,094 projects in 2023-24** (DST 53%, ICMR 23%, DBT 11%); the **academic sector received 73%** of the support; women's participation as principal investigators rose to **28% in 2023-24 from 13% in 2000-01**. — [DST NSTMIS, *R&D Statistics at a Glance 2025-26*, Charts 10–11, accessed 2026-11-20](https://www.dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf)

### Inferences
- The faculty-vacancy evidence and the researcher-stock evidence point the same way: India's R&D labour force is growing, but the senior academic posts that absorb and supervise PhDs have a large unfilled share (56% of professorial posts in central universities, January 2025). (Inference combining the 364th Report and DST Table 21, both accessed 2026-11-20.)
- The 700-grants/year cap on PM ECRG is small relative to a 5.07 lakh FTE researcher stock, but this is a *scale comparison*, not proof of unmet demand: no application/award-ratio data were found. (Inference from ANRF and DST figures cited above.)
- PhD "dropout" should not be quantified from the available public data: the AISHE 2021-22 enrolment-to-degree comparison cited by *The Hindu* is not a cohort measure and the 40% figure there is unnamed. (Inference from sources cited in Key Question 3.)

### Gaps
- No verified India-specific study on **early-career funding constraints** with attributable numbers was found on 2026-11-20; only scheme guidelines and a global INASP survey (~8,000 researchers, not India-specific) appeared in searches. Do not claim an early-career "funding crisis" from free sources without a named study. — [INASP/Global Young Academy survey blog, accessed 2026-11-20](https://council.science/blog/inasp-early-career-researchers-survey/)
- No free national dataset on **faculty vacancies by discipline** or **PhD supervisor availability**; the parliamentary report gives cadre-level totals only.
- **Later-report / paid-data gap only** (out of scope for the library): CMIE Prowess or DSIR microdata for firm-level R&D staff, Scopus/Web of Science or SciVal for researcher productivity and attrition, and paid patent/startup datasets. These are named as *gaps*, not used above.
