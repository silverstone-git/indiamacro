# Outcomes in India's education-to-R&D chain: what children learn, what spending can explain, and peer comparators

Access date for every live source linked below: **20 Nov 2026**. No paid source and no microdata-on-request file was used. Every number is labelled with the year/date of the observation or release. Unverified recollection is excluded from headline claims.

## 1. ASER: latest rural Std V reading/arithmetic, state variation, 2005–latest trend, definitions, pandemic gap and mode changes

### Takeaway
- The latest published **basic ASER** is **2024**, a **rural-only household survey**; it is not a national estimate including urban India ([ASER 2024 report, accessed 20 Nov 2026](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf); [ASER 2024 report index](https://asercentre.org/aser-2024/)). In 2024, **48.8%** of rural Std V children could read a Std II-level text and **30.7%** could solve a 3-digit-by-1-digit division problem. Reading recovered from **42.8% in 2022** but remained below the **50.5% in 2018** peak; division recovered from **25.6% in 2022** and exceeded **27.9% in 2018** ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- Government schools drove the rebound: rural Std V government-school reading rose **38.5% (2022) → 44.8% (2024)**, while private-school reading rose **56.8% → 59.3%**, still below **65.1% in 2018** ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- The comparable long run starts in **2006 for reading** and **2007 for arithmetic**: ASER's own trends report excludes **2005** because its sampling differed, and excludes **2006 arithmetic** because the tool changed ([ASER Trends Over Time 2006–2014](https://img.asercentre.org/docs/Publications/ASER%20Reports/ASER%20TOT/fullasertrendsovertimereport.pdf)). Do not splice 2005 into the headline trend.

### Cited Findings
- **Definitions (2024):** the reading tool asks whether a child can read letters, words, a Std-I-level paragraph, or a Std-II-level "story"; the child is scored at the highest level reached comfortably. Arithmetic levels are number recognition 1–9, 11–99, 2-digit subtraction with borrowing, and 3-digit-by-1-digit division. Tasks are administered one-on-one at home to children aged 5–16; the reading method has been unchanged since **2006** and arithmetic since **2006/2007** ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf); [ASER Trends Over Time](https://img.asercentre.org/docs/Publications/ASER%20Reports/ASER%20TOT/fullasertrendsovertimereport.pdf)).
- **2024 sample:** **649,491 children** in **17,997 villages** across **605 rural districts**, with **641 organisations** and **25,557 volunteers**; two-stage cluster design of **30 villages per district × 20 households = 600 households per district** ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- **All-India rural headline table (% able to reach the basic level):** [ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)

  | Measure | 2014 | 2016 | 2018 | 2022 | 2024 |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | Std V reading, Std II-level text, all schools | 48.0 | 47.9 | 50.5 | 42.8 | 48.8 |
  | Std V division, all schools | 26.1 | 26.0 | 27.9 | 25.6 | 30.7 |
  | Std III reading, Std II-level text, all schools | 23.6 | 25.1 | 27.2 | 20.5 | 27.1 |
  | Std III subtraction, all schools | 25.4 | 25.3 | 28.2 | 25.9 | 33.7 |

- **Earlier comparable trend:** Std V reading at Std II level was **53.1% (2006), 58.9% (2007), 56.3% (2008), 52.9% (2009), 53.7% (2010), 48.3% (2011), 46.9% (2012), 47.0% (2013), 48.1% (2014)**; Std V division was **42.5% (2007), 37.1% (2008), 38.1% (2009), 36.2% (2010), 27.6% (2011), 24.9% (2012), 25.6% (2013), 26.1% (2014)** ([ASER Trends Over Time](https://img.asercentre.org/docs/Publications/ASER%20Reports/ASER%20TOT/fullasertrendsovertimereport.pdf)).
- **State variation, 2024 rural government schools:** highest Std V reading was **Himachal Pradesh 70.1%**, **Punjab 66.0%** and **Mizoram 65.9%**; states with **>10 percentage-point** gains in Std V government-school reading over 2022–24 included **Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat and Tamil Nadu** ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- **Uttar Pradesh example (rural government schools):** Std III reading at Std II level rose **6.0% (2014) → 12.3% (2018) → 16.4% (2022) → 27.9% (2024)**; states with >10 pp Std III government-reading gains in 2022–24 included **Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Kerala, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Odisha and Maharashtra** ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- **Pandemic/mode break:** the basic ASER scheduled for **2020** was not conducted because of COVID restrictions; **national phone surveys were used in 2020 and 2021**. Fieldwork resumed in **2021** only in **Chhattisgarh, West Bengal and Karnataka**. **2022 and 2024 were in-person field rounds** ([ASER 2024 FAQ/Annexure 13](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Annexure_13.pdf); [ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- **Frame break:** ASER used the **Census 2001** village frame in 2005–2014 and **Census 2011** from 2016; **31 new rural districts** were added in 2011 and **10 new districts in Chhattisgarh in 2022**, so district-level series are not strictly comparable across the break ([ASER 2024 FAQ/Annexure 13](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Annexure_13.pdf); [ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).

### Inferences
- The 2022→2024 movement is a **broad rural recovery**, strongest in government schools and in arithmetic; it is **not** evidence that rural India had fully returned to 2018 learning levels by 2024, because Std V reading was still **1.7 pp** below 2018 ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- Because the assessment tool is unchanged, the 2006/2007–2024 direction is usable; because sampling frames and survey mode changed, **2005**, **2020** and **2021 phone** estimates must remain outside the main comparability chain ([ASER Trends Over Time](https://img.asercentre.org/docs/Publications/ASER%20Reports/ASER%20TOT/fullasertrendsovertimereport.pdf); [ASER 2024 FAQ](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Annexure_13.pdf)).
- Government–private gaps partly reflect **household self-selection**, not just school quality; ASER explicitly cautions against attributing the whole difference to school type ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).

### Gaps
- ASER is **rural-only**; it cannot support statements about urban India or a truly national child population ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- ASER microdata are available only on request; these notes use published tables only ([ASER Trends Over Time](https://img.asercentre.org/docs/Publications/ASER%20Reports/ASER%20TOT/fullasertrendsovertimereport.pdf)).
- 2020/21 phone-survey estimates are not interchangeable with in-person rounds, and only three states had 2021 field reports ([ASER 2024 FAQ](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Annexure_13.pdf)).
- ASER reports associations with FLN directives/training but no causal identification; see Question 4 ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).

## 2. NCERT NAS 2021 and PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024: grade 3/6/9 results by state and caveats

### Takeaway
- **NAS 2021** was a school-based, competency-based national assessment administered on **12 Nov 2021** in **1,18,274 schools** across **720 districts**, covering about **3.4 million students** in classes **III, V and VIII**, analysed with Item Response Theory (IRT) ([PARAKH NAS Dashboard](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/nas-dashboard); [NCERT interpretation report, Feb 2024](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NAS/nas_report_02_02_2024.pdf)).
- **PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan (PRS) 2024** was held on **4 Dec 2024** for classes **3, 6 and 9** using paper/OMR; the official site reports **74,229 schools**, **21,15,022 students**, **2,70,424 teachers**, **36 states/UTs** and **781 districts** ([PARAKH PRS 2024](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs); [Economic Survey 2025–26, Ch. 11](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap11.pdf)).
- Consistent national benchmarks from the 2024 state report cards are: **Grade 3 Language 64%, Mathematics 60%**; **Grade 6 Language 57%, Mathematics 46%, The World Around Us 49%**; **Grade 9 Language 54%, Mathematics 37%, Science 40%, Social Science 40%** ([Bihar PRS 2024 state card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Bihar.pdf); [Kerala PRS 2024 state card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Kerala.pdf)).
- State spread is wide: 2024 Grade 3 Language/Mathematics was **Punjab 82/78**, **Himachal Pradesh 76/72**, **Kerala 75/70**, versus **Bihar 56/54**, **Meghalaya 58/53** and **Tamil Nadu 58/55** ([Punjab card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Punjab.pdf); [Himachal Pradesh card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Himachal%20Pradesh.pdf); [Kerala card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Kerala.pdf); [Bihar card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Bihar.pdf); [Meghalaya card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Meghalaya.pdf); [Tamil Nadu card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Tamil%20Nadu.pdf)).
- **Conflict to flag:** Economic Survey 2025–26 says Grade III proficiency rose from **39% Language / 42% Mathematics in NAS 2021** to **57% Language / 65% Mathematics in PARAKH 2024**, but the official 2024 state cards show national **64% Language / 60% Mathematics**. The Survey's two 2024 numbers are therefore **not consistent with the state cards** and should not be used as the 2024 headline without reconciliation ([Economic Survey 2025–26, Ch. 11](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap11.pdf); [Bihar PRS 2024 card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Bihar.pdf)).

### Cited Findings
- **NAS 2021 method:** competency-based items, IRT scaling, performance levels, and pupil/school/teacher questionnaires that also captured pandemic-related learning and school context ([PARAKH NAS Dashboard](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/nas-dashboard); [NCERT interpretation report](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NAS/nas_report_02_02_2024.pdf)). NAS 2021 was the postponed **2020** cycle, administered in **November 2021** ([PARAKH NAS Dashboard](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/nas-dashboard)).
- **PRS 2024 design:** classes **3/6/9**, subjects Language, Mathematics, The World Around Us (grade 6), Science and Social Science (grade 9); **45 questions in grade 3, 51 in grade 6 and 60 in grade 9** ([PARAKH PRS 2024](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs)).
- **Participation-count discrepancy:** the PRS landing page reports **74,229 schools / 21,15,022 students**, while overview text on the same site refers to a planned **75,565 schools / over 22,94,377 students**; this is best read as **planned versus actual**, but the site does not explicitly reconcile it ([PARAKH PRS 2024](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs)).
- **2024 state cards, selected values (% correct):**

  | State | Grade 3 Lang/Math | Grade 6 Lang/Math/TWAU | Grade 9 Lang/Math/Sci/SS |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|
  | National (from Bihar/Kerala cards) | 64/60 | 57/46/49 | 54/37/40/40 |
  | Punjab | 82/78 | 74/62/64 | 69/52/54/52 |
  | Kerala | 75/70 | 76/60/66 | 74/45/53/51 |
  | Himachal Pradesh | 76/72 | 66/53/58 | 65/42/47/46 |
  | Bihar | 56/54 | 52/45/46 | 52/39/41/40 |
  | Meghalaya | 58/53 | 44/34/39 | 47/29/33/34 |
  | Tamil Nadu | 58/55 | 51/40/44 | 49/32/36/35 |

  Sources: [Bihar](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Bihar.pdf), [Kerala](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Kerala.pdf), [Punjab](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Punjab.pdf), [Himachal Pradesh](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Himachal%20Pradesh.pdf), [Meghalaya](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Meghalaya.pdf), [Tamil Nadu](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Tamil%20Nadu.pdf). Full list of 36 state/UT report cards: [PARAKH state list](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs-reports-2024-statelist).
- **Caveat printed in state cards:** "The mean difference has not been represented statistically"; state-versus-national gaps are descriptive, not significance tests ([Kerala PRS 2024 card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Kerala.pdf); [Bihar PRS 2024 card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Bihar.pdf)).
- **Economic Survey interpretation:** it says NAS 2021 showed schools relying on internal/board examinations that test recall, and recommends using assessment data diagnostically plus a possible PISA-like Grade X assessment ([Economic Survey 2025–26, Ch. 11](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap11.pdf)).

### Inferences
- NAS/PRS percentages are **school-based competency scores**, not the same construct as ASER's "can read Std II text" or "can divide"; do not put them on one chart or call one a continuation of the other ([PARAKH NAS Dashboard](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/nas-dashboard); [ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- The 2024 state spread is large even against one national benchmark, so a national average hides substantial state-level variation in implementation and contexts; the report cards do not identify causes ([PARAKH state list](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs-reports-2024-statelist); [Kerala PRS 2024 card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Kerala.pdf)).
- Use the **state-card national benchmarks** for 2024, and quote the Economic Survey's 2021→2024 rebound only as a **conflicting secondary comparison** ([Economic Survey 2025–26, Ch. 11](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap11.pdf); [Bihar PRS 2024 card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Bihar.pdf)).

### Gaps
- The **NAS 2021 National Report Card link** on the official dashboard did not resolve to a downloadable PDF when accessed on **20 Nov 2026**; state-level NAS 2021 values were not retrieved ([PARAKH NAS Dashboard](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/nas-dashboard)).
- The PRS 2024 technical report is scanned/poorly machine-readable, and state-card differences are not statistically tested ([PARAKH PRS 2024](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs); [Kerala PRS 2024 card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Kerala.pdf)).
- No public item-level microdata were used; results are aggregate report-card percentages ([PARAKH state list](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs-reports-2024-statelist)).
- Planned-versus-actual participation counts remain unreconciled on the official site ([PARAKH PRS 2024](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs)).

## 3. World Bank HCI, learning-adjusted years, HLO, learning poverty, India vs peers, and national PISA sample

### Takeaway
- **HCI 2020 (0–1):** India **0.494**, above Pakistan **0.406**, South Africa **0.425** and Bangladesh **0.464**, but below Indonesia **0.540**, Brazil **0.551** and China **0.653** ([World Bank HCI indicator, last updated 21 Sep 2020](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;BGD;PAK;CHN;BRA;IDN;ZAF/indicator/HD.HCI.OVRL?format=json&per_page=200); [HCI definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/HD.HCI.OVRL?format=json)). HCI measures the health-and-education contribution to a child's future worker productivity relative to full health and complete education ([HCI definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/HD.HCI.OVRL?format=json)).
- **HCI+ (2026 release, max 325):** India **159**, above the South Asia average **157** and lower-middle-income average **153**, but below Indonesia **175**, Brazil **203**, China **220**; Pakistan is **130**, Bangladesh **147**, South Africa **132** ([India brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099841307032634415/pdf/IDU-b6ea9ac1-a7a9-4e54-82e1-92b54ec783d7.pdf); [Indonesia brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099357406262636672/pdf/IDU-a9344115-da63-4e64-b494-b7792886c342.pdf); [Brazil brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099148506302615249/pdf/IDU-d9038768-dc16-4023-8752-3a18f5c419fa.pdf); [China brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099337006262615341/pdf/IDU-9bc349ad-9332-4a93-b41d-251f3ebcd392.pdf); [Pakistan brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099003106242642062/pdf/IDU-94d5adf3-97ae-49d5-b71a-86b1be9d8e15.pdf); [Bangladesh brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099713207032614910/pdf/IDU-77304f37-2417-4bdd-ba7a-4b9d9cd1c948.pdf); [South Africa brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099446206262625745/pdf/IDU-4ae7771b-d799-4e93-870a-a8e7164c978c.pdf)). HCI+ extends HCI to tertiary education and work experience ([HCI+ 2026 methodology note](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/8c011df6-2134-4da6-8f40-160ce2cf37a4)).
- **Learning-adjusted years of school (LAYS, 2020):** India **7.10** years, above Bangladesh **5.99**, Pakistan **5.08** and South Africa **5.60**, but below Brazil **7.87**, Indonesia **7.83** and China **9.27** ([World Bank LAYS indicator, last updated 21 Sep 2020](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;BGD;PAK;CHN;BRA;IDN;ZAF/indicator/HD.HCI.LAYS?format=json&per_page=200)). LAYS = expected years of school × (most recent harmonised test score / **625**) ([LAYS definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/HD.HCI.LAYS?format=json)). Harmonised Learning Outcomes (HLO) is the common-scale test input used in this adjustment and in HCI+ ([LAYS definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/HD.HCI.LAYS?format=json); [India HCI+ brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099841307032634415/pdf/IDU-b6ea9ac1-a7a9-4e54-82e1-92b54ec783d7.pdf)).
- **Learning poverty:** the current World Bank indicator has **no India value** as accessed **20 Nov 2026**; it reports **Bangladesh 51.22% (2022)** and **Brazil 46.90% (2019)** ([World Bank learning-poverty indicator, last updated 11 Nov 2026](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;BGD;PAK;CHN;BRA;IDN;ZAF/indicator/SE.LPV.PRIM?format=json&per_page=200)). It is the share of 10-year-olds who cannot read and understand a short age-appropriate passage, combining out-of-school children with below-minimum-proficiency in-school children ([learning-poverty definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/SE.LPV.PRIM?format=json)).
- **PISA:** India has **no nationally representative PISA sample** in PISA 2022. India's only PISA round was **2009**, and even that was not a national sample: the Indian participants were from **Tamil Nadu and Himachal Pradesh**; India then stayed out of **2012, 2015, 2018 and 2022** ([PISA India history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programme_for_International_Student_Assessment#India); [Education in India, 2009 state participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_India); [OECD PISA 2022](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/programmes/pisa/pisa-2022.html)).

### Cited Findings
- HCI+'s three pillars are health, education and employment; India's 2026 brief reports pillar scores **39 health / 97 education / 23 employment (max 325)**, and names **tertiary completion, HLO and expected years of schooling** as the largest contributors to India's gap with China ([India HCI+ brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099841307032634415/pdf/IDU-b6ea9ac1-a7a9-4e54-82e1-92b54ec783d7.pdf)).
- HCI+ is a **2026 release** and is not the same series as HCI 2020; the two should be presented side by side with their dates, not joined as one trend ([HCI 2020 indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND/indicator/HD.HCI.OVRL?format=json); [HCI+ 2026 methodology](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/8c011df6-2134-4da6-8f40-160ce2cf37a4)).
- ASER (rural, 2024) and World Bank LAYS/HLO (2020/2026 releases) measure different populations and constructs; ASER is a direct basic-literacy test, while LAYS/HLO rescale existing test scores against a common international benchmark ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf); [LAYS definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/HD.HCI.LAYS?format=json)).

### Inferences
- India sits **mid-pack among the selected peers**: ahead of South Asian comparators on HCI/LAYS, well behind China and Brazil on HCI/HCI+, and with no current comparable World Bank learning-poverty value ([HCI indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;BGD;PAK;CHN;BRA;IDN;ZAF/indicator/HD.HCI.OVRL?format=json&per_page=200); [LAYS indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;BGD;PAK;CHN;BRA;IDN;ZAF/indicator/HD.HCI.LAYS?format=json&per_page=200); [learning-poverty indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;BGD;PAK;CHN;BRA;IDN;ZAF/indicator/SE.LPV.PRIM?format=json&per_page=200)).
- The HCI+ brief's diagnosis points to **learning, tertiary completion and work** as joint constraints; it does not say that one input, such as school spending, is the sole cause ([India HCI+ brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099841307032634415/pdf/IDU-b6ea9ac1-a7a9-4e54-82e1-92b54ec783d7.pdf)).
- Without a national PISA sample, India cannot be placed on PISA's 15-year-old scale from official PISA data; ASER/NAS/PRS are the domestic evidence base, with different ages and sampling ([PISA India history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programme_for_International_Student_Assessment#India); [PARAKH NAS Dashboard](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/nas-dashboard)).

### Gaps
- No current India value in the World Bank learning-poverty API; do not substitute ASER rural percentages for the World Bank definition ([learning-poverty indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;BGD;PAK;CHN;BRA;IDN;ZAF/indicator/SE.LPV.PRIM?format=json&per_page=200); [definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/SE.LPV.PRIM?format=json)).
- HCI/HCI+/LAYS use different vintages (2020 indicator data vs 2026 HCI+ briefs); they are comparators, not a single time series ([HCI indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND/indicator/HD.HCI.OVRL?format=json); [HCI+ methodology](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/8c011df6-2134-4da6-8f40-160ce2cf37a4)).
- No public microdata were used for HLO or PISA India; HLO is a-harmonised aggregate input in World Bank indices ([LAYS definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/HD.HCI.LAYS?format=json)).

## 4. Honest spending–learning link and claims correlations cannot support

### Takeaway
- India's **general government expenditure on education** was **4.10% of GDP in 2022**, up from **3.38% in 2010**, but below Brazil **5.62% (2022)** and South Africa **6.16% (2022)** and above Bangladesh **2.09% (2022)** and Pakistan **1.97% (2022)** ([World Bank/UNESCO expenditure indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;BGD;PAK;CHN;BRA;IDN;ZAF/indicator/SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=200); [definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS?format=json)). These are input shares, not measures of learning ([expenditure definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS?format=json)).
- Over the same broad period, India's LAYS rose from **5.78 (2017) to 7.10 (2020)**, but cross-country spending and LAYS rankings do not line up mechanically: South Africa spent **6.17% of GDP in 2020** with LAYS **5.60**, while India spent **4.29%** with LAYS **7.10** ([expenditure indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;ZAF/indicator/SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=200); [LAYS indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;ZAF/indicator/HD.HCI.LAYS?format=json&per_page=200)). That is a **descriptive comparison**, not evidence of causality or efficiency ([World Bank WDR 2018](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/wdr2018)).
- ASER's 2022→24 rural gains coincided with the national FLN push: ASER reports **83%** of sampled schools received an FLN directive, **78%** had at least one FLN-trained teacher and **75%** received FLN teaching-learning material in 2024. The report calls the gains "unprecedented" and points to NEP 2020/NIPUN Bharat, but its design is observational, so this is **association, not proof of effect** ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).

### Cited Findings
- World Bank WDR 2018 frames the core problem as putting **learning** at the centre and making schools and systems work for learners, not merely adding inputs ([World Bank WDR 2018](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/wdr2018)).
- Economic Survey 2025–26 highlights non-spending correlates of rural learning: the share of mothers of children aged 3–8 with no schooling fell **43% (2014) → 24% (2024)**, and the share of underage children (five or below) in Grade I fell **25.6% (2018) → 16.7% (2024)** ([Economic Survey 2025–26, Ch. 11](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap11.pdf)).
- ASER warns that government–private differences reflect household self-selection; even after controlling for household characteristics a private-school edge remains, but the whole gap cannot be called a "school effect" ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- PARAKH state cards show large within-country variation under one national system; for example Grade 3 Language ranged from **82% in Punjab to 56% in Bihar in 2024**, which is not explained by a single national spending figure ([Punjab PRS card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Punjab.pdf); [Bihar PRS card](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/media/public_files/2025-08/PRS%202024%20Bihar.pdf)).

### Inferences
- A defensible claim is: **"India increased education spending and foundational learning improved after 2022, and FLN programmes were widely reported in schools."** The sources document those facts separately ([expenditure indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND/indicator/SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=100); [ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- Claims that **cannot** be made from these correlations: (i) the extra spending **caused** the 2022–24 ASER gains; (ii) NIPUN Bharat/NEP **caused** the gains; (iii) private schools are intrinsically better because their students score higher; (iv) a country that spends more per GDP must learn more; (v) India's HCI/HCI+ gap with China is entirely an education-budget gap ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf); [World Bank WDR 2018](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/wdr2018); [India HCI+ brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099841307032634415/pdf/IDU-b6ea9ac1-a7a9-4e54-82e1-92b54ec783d7.pdf)).
- To support a causal statement, one would need a design that isolates the intervention (randomised rollout, credible quasi-experiment, or household/school panel with adequate controls); none of the free aggregate sources used here provides that ([ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf); [Economic Survey 2025–26, Ch. 11](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap11.pdf)).

### Gaps
- No linked district-level panel joining education expenditure to ASER or PARAKH outcomes was found in free public sources; this note therefore reports association only ([World Bank/UNESCO expenditure indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND/indicator/SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=100); [ASER 2024 report](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)).
- Education-spending series have country breaks (for example Indonesia's reported share drops sharply after 2015), so peer spending comparisons need year-by-year checks ([World Bank/UNESCO expenditure indicator](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IDN/indicator/SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=100)).
- ASER microdata, NAS/PRS microdata and PLFS microdata are on-request sources and were deliberately excluded; they are the next step for covariate or value-added analysis ([ASER Trends Over Time](https://img.asercentre.org/docs/Publications/ASER%20Reports/ASER%20TOT/fullasertrendsovertimereport.pdf); [PARAKH NAS Dashboard](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/nas-dashboard)).
