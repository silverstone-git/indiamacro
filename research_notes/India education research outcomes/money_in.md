# Money in: public and private funding of R&D and education in India, with peer comparators

Scope note: all live facts below were accessed on **20 Nov 2026**. Year labels are reproduced exactly as the source gives them (Indian sources usually use fiscal years, World Bank/OECD/UNESCO use calendar years). ₹ crore = 10 million rupees; 1 lakh crore = ₹10 trillion. The notes deliberately separate **allocation**, **actual expenditure** and **utilisation documentation**; none of the latter implies misuse.

## 1. India's GERD as % of GDP: latest value, 10–15 year trend, peers, and series breaks

### Takeaway
India's latest official GERD is **0.84% of GDP in 2023-24** (₹2,44,767.81 crore), from DST/NSTMIS *Research & Development Statistics 2025-26* (July 2026); the World Bank series stops at **0.646% in 2020**, so it understates the current level by ~0.2 pp and must not be used as "latest". Real GERD has more than tripled since 2013-14, but the ratio has been flat-to-down over the full 15-year window and remains far below Korea (~4.9%), the US (~3.4%), Germany (~3.2%), the UK (~2.7%) and China (~2.6%).

### Cited Findings

**India — official DST/NSTMIS series (primary, fiscal years)**
- GERD rose from **₹79,355.89 crore in 2013-14 to ₹2,44,767.81 crore in 2023-24**; GERD/GDP reached **0.84% in 2023-24 "for the first time"**, up from 0.64% in 2020-21. NSTMIS labels **2024-25 (₹2,76,196.13 crore; 0.87%) and 2025-26 (₹3,13,007.02 crore; 0.90%) as *estimated*, not actual** — do not report them as outcomes. — [NSTMIS, *R&D Statistics at a Glance 2025-26*, July 2026, pp. 1–2](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Same source, Chart 1: **0.71% in 1990-91** — i.e. the 2023-24 peak is only ~0.13 pp above the level three decades earlier. GERD in current PPP dollars: **US$57.9 bn in 2020-21 → US$120.6 bn in 2023-24**. — [NSTMIS 2025-26, pp. 1–2](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Parliamentary answer (Rajya Sabha UNQ 2204, answered **6 Aug 2026**) gives the exact five-year official series (₹ crore / % of GDP): **2019-20 1,32,567.01 / 0.66; 2020-21 1,27,380.96 / 0.64; 2021-22 1,94,810.83 / 0.83; 2022-23 2,13,378.12 / 0.82; 2023-24 2,44,767.81 / 0.84**. The 2020-21 absolute fall is visible in the data. — [DST, Rajya Sabha UNQ 2204, 6 Aug 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/271/AU2204_8RZMVe.pdf?source=pqars), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Government's own stated policy benchmark: **2% of GDP by 2035**; the same answer notes GERD rose 85% in absolute terms from FY20 to FY24 while the ratio moved only 0.66% → 0.84%. — [Business Standard, 30 Jul 2026, reporting the Lok Sabha reply](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/r-d-spending-in-india-only-0-84-of-gdp-in-fy24-govt-tells-parliament-126073001491_1.html), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

**India — World Bank series (GB.XPD.RSDV.GD.ZS; API `lastupdated` 2026-11-11)**
- India's World Bank series **ends in 2020 at 0.64558%**, with no 2021–2025 observations. Selected earlier values: 2005 0.82411; 2008 0.85876; 2012 0.74399; 2014 0.70158; 2016 0.66984; 2018 0.66001; 2019 0.65942; 2020 0.64558. — [World Bank API, GB.XPD.RSDV.GD.ZS, IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA, 2005–2026](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA/indicator/GB.XPD.RSDV.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=1000&date=2005:2026), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- **Series alignment:** World Bank 2019 (0.659) is numerically consistent with DST's 2019-20 (0.66), so this is a **lag/coverage break, not a contradictory estimate**. Calendar-year vs fiscal-year denominators and different compilation dates are the likely reason; the sources do not publish a reconciliation. — [World Bank API (above)](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA/indicator/GB.XPD.RSDV.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=1000&date=2005:2026) and [DST Rajya Sabha answer, 6 Aug 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/271/AU2204_8RZMVe.pdf?source=pqars), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

**Peers, 2023 (same World Bank indicator and API pull)**
- **Korea 4.94352%; USA 3.44716%; Germany 3.15390%; UK 2.67614%; China 2.57729%; Brazil 1.19380%**. — [World Bank API, GB.XPD.RSDV.GD.ZS, 2005–2026](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA/indicator/GB.XPD.RSDV.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=1000&date=2005:2026), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- DST's own Chart 8 (source: UNESCO website accessed **21 Jun 2026**, India from DST) gives, for 2023: **Brazil 1.19%, Russia 0.94%, China 2.58%, South Africa 0.61%** and says most developed countries spent >2%. These agree with World Bank for Brazil and China to 2 decimals. — [NSTMIS 2025-26, p. 8](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Trend comparison over the World Bank window: Korea 2.43% (2005) → 4.94% (2023); China 1.29% → 2.58%; Germany 2.40% → 3.15%; India 0.82% (2005) → 0.65% (2020). — [World Bank API (above)](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA/indicator/GB.XPD.RSDV.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=1000&date=2005:2026), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

**Method/provenance of the Indian number**
- NSTMIS has run the National S&T Survey since 1973-74; the 2024-25 round was conducted **Dec 2024–Dec 2025**, covered **>8,000 R&D-performing institutions** in six sectors, used questionnaires aligned to the **OECD Frascati Manual (2015)**, and was validated by an expert committee. — [NSTMIS 2025-26, "Overview"](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Survey responses were supplemented with **authenticated secondary sources including CMIE, annual reports, BRSR reports, Public Enterprises Survey, NIRF, AISHE, DSIR records and Demand for Grants** — important because it means the published aggregate uses paid databases even though the aggregate itself is free. — [NSTMIS 2025-26, "Overview"](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

### Inferences
- For a student-facing dataset, the defensible "latest India" value is **DST 0.84% (FY24)**; the defensible "latest World Bank-comparable" value is **0.646% (2020)**. Showing both, with dates, is essential — substituting one for the other is a 0.2 pp error.
- The 15-year story is "rapid nominal growth, weak intensity convergence": absolute GERD grew ~3x from FY14, but the ratio in FY24 is only marginally above 1990-91 and below the world's major R&D economies.
- Do not treat NSTMIS projections for FY25/FY26 as observations.

### Gaps
- No free, machine-readable year-by-year DST GERD table was retrieved: Chart 1 is a graphic and the companion *S&T Indicators Tables 2025-26* PDF did not download intact. Year-wise values are available only for FY20–FY24 via the Parliament answer. The 1990-91 and other intermediate chart points are not tabulated in the retrieved text.
- No source located that reconciles DST fiscal-year GERD with the World Bank/UNESCO calendar-year series; the break is documented here as a lag/definition issue, not explained.
- No free constant-price GERD series with explicit base year was available (Chart 2 reports growth rates only).

## 2. Government education expenditure as % of GDP, peer values, and the NEP 6% target

### Takeaway
There are **three incompatible-looking but definitionally different public education series**: World Bank/UNESCO 4.10% of GDP (India, 2022); Ministry of Education's *Analysis of Budgeted Expenditure on Education* 4.06% (Centre + States, 2022-23) and 4.12% (2021-22); and Economic Survey 2025-26 / RBI 2.7% (Centre + States, FY26 BE, "Education, Sports, Art and Culture"). **All are far below the NEP 2020 goal of 6% of GDP**, which has no binding date ("at the earliest") and is not close to being met.

### Cited Findings

**World Bank SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS (government expenditure on education, % GDP; API `lastupdated` 2026-11-11)**
- India, full available series: 2005 3.19; 2010 3.38; 2015 4.11; 2018 4.36; 2019 3.90; 2020 4.29; 2021 4.63; **2022 4.10 (latest)**. — [World Bank API, SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS, IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA, 2005–2026](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA/indicator/SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=1000&date=2005:2026), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Latest peer values available in the same pull (years differ — do not present as a single-year cross-section): **Brazil 5.62% (2022); China 3.90% (2023); Germany 5.24% (2022); UK 5.91% (2021); US 5.42% (2021); Korea 5.41% (2022)**. — [World Bank API, SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND;CHN;KOR;USA;GBR;DEU;BRA/indicator/SE.XPD.TOTL.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=1000&date=2005:2026), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

**Indian official series A — Ministry of Education, *Analysis of Budgeted Expenditure on Education***
- As per *Analysis of Budgeted Expenditure on Education, 2022-23*, **public expenditure on education (Centre + States/UTs) = 4.06% of GDP**. — [Lok Sabha UNQ 2642, answered 9 Mar 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU2642_2m08h4.pdf?source=pqals), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Earlier edition figures, tabled 17 Dec 2025: **2019-20 Actual 4.04%; 2020-21 RE 4.36%; 2021-22 BE 4.12%** of GDP. Absolute totals (₹ crore): **8,12,214 (2019-20); 8,64,452 (2020-21 RE); 9,67,177 (2021-22 BE)**. Sector split for 2021-22 BE: elementary/primary 1.79%, secondary 1.04%, university/higher 0.52%, technical 0.77%, adult ~0.00%. — [Rajya Sabha UNQ 1993, answered 17 Dec 2025](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/269/AU1993_wjzj6C.pdf?source=pqars), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Note the internal labelling caveat: the 2021-22 figure is a **BE**, not an actual, and the 2020-21 figure is an **RE** — the "4.x%" series mixes budget and revised stages. — [Rajya Sabha UNQ 1993](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/269/AU1993_wjzj6C.pdf?source=pqars), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

**Indian official series B — Economic Survey 2025-26 / RBI (different definition)**
- Economic Survey 2025-26, Chapter 13, chart: **general-government (Centre + States) "Expenditure on Education" as % of GDP: FY19 2.8, FY20 2.9, FY21 2.9, FY22 2.7, FY23 2.7, FY24 2.7, FY25 RE 2.7, FY26 BE 2.7**. Source: RBI; GDP at current market prices, 2011-12 base; FY26 GDP per Union Budget FY26. — [Economic Survey 2025-26, Chapter 13, "Rural Development and Social Progress"](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap13.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- The same chapter explicitly defines the head: **"Expenditure on 'Education' pertains to expenditure on Education, Sports, Arts and Culture."** This is the key to the 2.7% vs 4.1% conflict — different aggregations, not necessarily rival measurements. — [Economic Survey 2025-26, Chapter 13, note 18](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap13.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- RBI *State Finances: A Study of Budgets of 2025-26*, Statement 26: **All States and UTs education expenditure = 2.5% of GDP in 2024-25 RE and 2.5% in 2025-26 BE**, and **13.1% of aggregate state expenditure in 2025-26 BE** (down from 15.1% in 2019-20). — [RBI, Statement 26, 23 Jan 2026](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/Publications/PDFs/26_ST23012026EEBC26225EBB446BB92149E55FCBF22C.PDF), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

**NEP 2020 6% target — status**
- NEP 2020's formulation, as quoted by government: "The Centre and the States will work together to increase the public investment in Education sector to reach 6% of GDP **at the earliest**" — no dated deadline. — [Rajya Sabha UNQ 3220, answered 29 Mar 2023](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/259/AU3220.pdf?source=pqars), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- As recently as **9 Mar 2026**, the Ministry answered a question on the 6% target by citing 4.06% (2022-23) and stating that Centre and States are "working together to increase the public investment in education sector to reach 6% ... at the earliest"; no financing gap or bridge plan was tabled. — [Lok Sabha UNQ 2642, 9 Mar 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU2642_2m08h4.pdf?source=pqals), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Ministry of Education budget alone: **₹93,224.31 crore (2021-22) → ₹1,39,289.48 crore (2026-27), +49.4% in five years**; the Ministry also notes education is a Concurrent List subject and State budgets are separate. — [Lok Sabha UNQ 2642, 9 Mar 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU2642_2m08h4.pdf?source=pqals), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- PRS records that the **Standing Committee on Education (2025) recommended an 8–10% increase** in the Ministry's allocation to keep pace with inflation, and that the Ministry's expenditure grew at a **5% CAGR between 2017-18 and 2024-25**. — [PRS, Demand for Grants 2026-27 Analysis: Education](https://prsindia.org/budgets/parliament/demand-for-grants-2026-27-analysis-education), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

### Inferences
- Report the level with its definition attached: "4.06% of GDP (MoE, Centre+States, 2022-23 BE-based)" and "2.7% of GDP (Economic Survey/RBI, general government, education+sports+art+culture, FY26 BE)" are both citable; a single undated "India spends X% on education" is not.
- On every official series the 6% target remains unmet, and the gap is ~1.9–3.3 pp depending on definition. No dated operational roadmap exists in the sources retrieved.
- Peer comparisons are indicative only: latest available years range from 2021 to 2023, and World Bank education data are compiled from UNESCO/UIS with country-specific lags.

### Gaps
- No free source giving a consistent, single-definition Centre+States education-to-GDP series through FY25/FY26 actuals; the 4.x% series ends at 2022-23 and the 2.7% series is budget-based.
- No government-published costing of the additional resources needed to reach 6%, nor a date for it.
- Secondary press reports (e.g. *India Today*, 29 Jul 2025, "2.9% in 2024-25"; *ET Education*, 29 Jul 2026, "2.7% FY26 BE") were seen but are not used for headline claims; the Economic Survey is the primary source for the 2.7% figure.

## 3. Who funds and performs R&D in India; the business share; what free data can and cannot support

### Takeaway
In FY24 government remained the largest **performer/funder bloc** (~48% of GERD on the narrow government definition), but **private industry alone was 45.2%** and **"private sector" including SIROs and private HEIs was 51.8%** — the source of an apparent but definitional conflict. Within central government R&D, DRDO, ISRO/DoS, DAE and ICAR dominate (76% of central sector R&D comes from 12 agencies). Free data stop at the sector aggregate; **no free firm-level R&D dataset exists** — CMIE Prowess and similar paid products are out of scope here and must be named as a gap.

### Cited Findings

**Sector split, FY24 vs FY21 (NSTMIS, primary)**
- FY24 shares: **private sector industry 45.2%; central sector institutions/labs 30.9%; higher education institutions 12.6%; public sector industry 4.1%; state government institutions 4.0%; SIROs 3.2%**. FY21 comparison: **32.2% / 43.7% / 8.8% / 4.4% / 6.7% / 4.2%** respectively. — [NSTMIS 2025-26, Chart 3, p. 3](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Broader three-way grouping (Chart 5): **government sector 54.5 / 52.0 / 48.2%; private industry 39.7 / 41.6 / 45.2%; others (SIROs + private HEIs) 5.8 / 6.4 / 6.6%** for FY22, FY23, FY24. The source says government still remains the primary contributor and private-industry R&D grew ~20% a year on average over FY22–FY24. — [NSTMIS 2025-26, Chart 5, p. 5](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

**Resolving the 45.2% vs 51.8% conflict**
- DST's Rajya Sabha answer (6 Aug 2026) defines **"Private Sector" = private industry + SIROs + private HEIs**, giving **₹1,26,906.60 crore of ₹2,44,767.81 crore = 51.8% in 2023-24**; "Government Sector" = central ministries/departments + state S&T institutions/agricultural universities + public/joint sector industry + public HEIs = **₹1,17,861.21 crore**. — [DST, Rajya Sabha UNQ 2204, 6 Aug 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/271/AU2204_8RZMVe.pdf?source=pqars), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Full five-year split on that definition (₹ crore): Government **87,813.47 / 80,992.83 / 1,06,133.32 / 1,10,949.77 / 1,17,861.21**; Private **44,753.54 / 46,388.13 / 88,677.51 / 1,02,428.35 / 1,26,906.60** for FY20–FY24. — [DST, Rajya Sabha UNQ 2204](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/271/AU2204_8RZMVe.pdf?source=pqars), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Secondary reporting of "51.8%, first time private > government" is consistent with this broader DST definition, while "45.2%" refers to private *industry only*. — [GSTimes, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.gstimes.in/indias-rd-expenditure-suged-to-0-84-in-2023-24/), accessed 20 Nov 2026; [NSTMIS Chart 3](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

**Central government performers and industry composition**
- **76% of central sector R&D expenditure in FY24 came from 12 major scientific agencies.** Shares within those 12: **DRDO 32.7%, Department of Space 15.0%, DAE 14.4%, ICAR 13.1%, CSIR 8.7%, DST 5.0%, ICMR 3.3%, MeitY 2.7%, DBT 2.1%, MoES 1.9%, MoEFCC 0.9%, MNRE 0.1%**. — [NSTMIS 2025-26, Chart 4, p. 4](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Industrial R&D by field, FY24: **transportation 32.6%, drugs & pharmaceuticals 21.4%, biotechnology 8.6%, IT 7.9%, electricals & electronics 5.9%**. Public sector industrial R&D was led by defence, fuels, industrial equipment and electricals/electronics. — [NSTMIS 2025-26, Chart 6, p. 6](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- **MNCs accounted for 71.3% of business R&D expenditure in India in FY24** (from 49.9% in FY21; 68.8% FY22; 67.6% FY23), from 252 private Indian MNCs, 124 private foreign MNCs and 14 public Indian MNCs. — [NSTMIS 2025-26, Chart 7, p. 7](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Extramural R&D support by central S&T agencies: **₹3,356.36 crore in FY24 vs ₹2,002.70 crore in FY21**, only **1.4% of GERD**; **DST 53%, ICMR 23%, DBT 11%, MeitY 4%, DRDO 4%**; academic institutions received **73%**. — [NSTMIS 2025-26, Chart 10, p. 10](https://dst.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/07/ab7aa62b590dcc3a18f6dabde045b5a0.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Universities' share of India's research spending was **9% in 2020-21**, lower than several comparator countries — note this is a different year/source from NSTMIS's 12.6% HEI share in FY24. — [PRS, Demand for Grants 2026-27 Analysis: Education, citing *R&D Statistics at a Glance 2022-23*](https://prsindia.org/budgets/parliament/demand-for-grants-2026-27-analysis-education), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

### Inferences
- Use the narrow sector table (45.2% private industry) for "who performs R&D" and the broad government/private table (51.8% private) only with its explicit definition. Both are DST numbers; the difference is SIROs + private HEIs (~6.6%).
- "Business R&D" is not the same as "private R&D": NSTMIS's MNC statistic covers 71.3% of *business* R&D, including public-sector Indian MNCs, and cannot be read as "foreign firms fund 71% of Indian R&D".
- Everything below the sector level (firm identity, concentration, R&D intensity by company, who actually decides how private R&D is spent) is out of reach with free public sources.

### Gaps
- **No free firm-level R&D data.** NSTMIS itself used **CMIE** and **BRSR/annual reports** to fill private-sector gaps but publishes only aggregates. CMIE Prowess, proprietary company databases and paid platforms are **named here as a gap only**, not used. This is the single largest limitation for any claim about private R&D concentration or drivers.
- No free state-wise GERD series; state government institutions are a single 4.0% line.
- No free public series separating foreign-owned MNC R&D from Indian MNC R&D within the 71.3% aggregate.
- No free data on R&D performed by not-for-profit/other sectors beyond the SIRO residual.

The free primary source is the **Expenditure Budget "Statement 3" (stat3a.pdf/xlsx)**, which gives Actuals 2024-25, BE 2025-26, RE 2025-26 and BE 2026-27 for every demand. ### Inferences
- The cleanest way to show "allocated vs spent" is Statement 3's four-column table; it is free, official and machine-readable (stat3a.xlsx is also published). Scheme-level utilisation must come from PIB/Parliament answers and is not available as a single tidy series.
- Large BE→RE cuts in science budgets are concentrated in **new, mission-style capital schemes (RDI in particular)**, while core departmental schemes in the PIB 2024-25 table were >90% utilised. This is a statement about budget mechanics, not about corruption or research quality.
- Outstanding UCs and idle equipment are **audit/compliance findings on documentation and asset use**; they support a caveat about measurement and execution capacity, not a claim that allocations were embezzled.

### Gaps
- No free consolidated table of **scheme-wise funds surrendered** by science or education departments. Rajya Sabha UNQ 1993 (17 Dec 2025) explicitly asked for BE/RE and funds surrendered since 2019-20; the answer supplied only department-level BE/RE/Actuals and did not provide the scheme-wise surrender data.
- No free dataset of utilisation by state for centrally sponsored science/education schemes.
- No free, consistent BE/RE/Actuals series for DRDO separated from the Ministry of Defence's four demands; PIB and MoD Annual Report give BE-level figures only.
- ANRF private contributions are reported as **letters of intent/estimates**, not realised receipts, so they cannot be counted as money actually in the system.

## 5. Major new funding institutions and policies (dates and amounts)

### Takeaway
Three changes dominate the period: **ANRF** (statutory body, in force Feb 2024, ₹50,000 crore mobilisation target of which ₹14,000 crore Centre), the **₹1 lakh crore RDI scheme** (Cabinet-approved 1 Jul 2025, operationalised through an ANRF special purpose fund) and large mission/semiconductor outlays. Amounts announced as multi-year envelopes must not be read as annual budget expenditure.

### Cited Findings
- **Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF):** established under the **Anusandhan National Research Foundation Act, 2023 (Act 25 of 2023)**, which **came into force on 5 February 2024**; target to **mobilise ₹50,000 crore over 2023–28**, of which **₹14,000 crore from the Central Government** and the balance expected from non-government sources (industry, philanthropists). — [PIB, "India's Leap in Research and Innovation", 4 Nov 2025, PRID 2186327](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2186327&noshow=1), accessed 20 Nov 2026. The same ₹14,000 crore central provision is repeated in [DST's Rajya Sabha answer, 6 Aug 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/271/AU2204_8RZMVe.pdf?source=pqars), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- ANRF's annual budget line inside DST: **₹2,000 crore BE 2025-26 → ₹1,948 crore RE 2025-26 → ₹2,000 crore BE 2026-27**; SERB (which ANRF replaced) **₹693.25 cr BE → ₹543.25 cr RE/BE**. — [India Budget, Demand 89 notes](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/eb/sbe89.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- **RDI scheme / fund:** total outlay **₹1 lakh crore over six years**, approved by the **Union Cabinet on 1 July 2025**, DST as nodal department; long-term low/nil-interest financing, equity for startups and contributions to a Deep-Tech Fund of Funds, targeting **TRL 4 and above**, up to **50% of assessed project cost**; a **Special Purpose Fund within ANRF** is first-level custodian, with second-level fund managers (AIFs, DFIs, NBFCs, FROs such as TDB and BIRAC). — [DST, RDI Cell](https://dst.gov.in/rdi-scheme/research-development-and-innovation-rdi-cell), accessed 20 Nov 2026; [PIB, ANRF Executive Council approval of SPF and guidelines, 11 Oct 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2177845), accessed 20 Nov 2026; [PIB, Cabinet approval, PRID 2153545](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2153545), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- RDI operational status: **first cohort of 19 second-level fund managers approved 8 Oct 2026 (14 AIFs + 5 FROs) with total RDIF commitment ₹7,131 crore**. — [PIB, 12 Oct 2026, PRID 2322295](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2322295&lang=2&reg=48), accessed 20 Nov 2026. The RDI website lists **21 SLFMs empanelled** and a Gazette notification of **ANRF (utilisation of RDIF) Financial Rules, 2026** dated Nov 2026. — [RDI Fund website](https://rdifund.anrf.gov.in/), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- ANRF private mobilisation remains only at Letter-of-Intent stage: Gates Foundation **~₹450 crore (USD 10m/yr for five years, plus a possible USD 25m second phase)**, Wadhwani Foundation **₹100 crore**, Google **~₹54 crore (USD 6m)**. — [PIB, 29 Jul 2026, PRID 2291131](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2291131&lang=2&reg=48), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Other mission envelopes cited by DST to Parliament: **National Quantum Mission ₹6,003.65 crore; National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems ₹3,660 crore; India Semiconductor Mission ₹76,000 crore**; also National Supercomputing Mission, NIDHI, BIRAC, iDEX, TIDE 2.0. — [DST, Rajya Sabha UNQ 2204, 6 Aug 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/271/AU2204_8RZMVe.pdf?source=pqars), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- Semiconductor/electronics budget detail: **Semicon India: ₹638 crore actual 2024-25 → ₹4,300 crore RE 2025-26 → ₹8,000 crore BE 2026-27**; **ECMS outlay ₹22,919 crore for six years, proposed to rise to ₹40,000 crore, with ₹1,500 crore allocated in 2026-27**; PRS notes underutilisation of PLI for IT hardware, Semicon India and IndiaAI Mission funds. — [PRS, Demand for Grants 2026-27 Analysis: Electronics and Information Technology](https://prsindia.org/budgets/parliament/demand-for-grants-2025-26-analysis-electronics-and-information-technology), accessed 20 Nov 2026. MeitY's total allocation fell from **₹26,026.25 crore BE 2025-26 to ₹21,632.96 crore BE 2026-27**. — [India Budget, Statement 3](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/eb/stat3a.pdf), accessed 20 Nov 2026.
- **Biopharma SHAKTI:** new five-year scheme with **₹10,000 crore outlay**, announced in Union Budget 2026-27. — [PRS, Union Budget 2026-27 Analysis](https://prsindia.org/budgets/parliament/union-budget-2026-27-analysis), accessed 20 Nov 2026; DBT Secretary's description in [Artifex.News, 11 Feb 2026](https://artifex.news/article70619954-ece), accessed 20 Nov 2026.

### Inferences
- Multi-year envelopes (ANRF ₹50,000 cr, RDI ₹1 lakh cr, ISM ₹76,000 cr) are **policy announcements, not annual expenditures**; only the annual budget lines (e.g. RDI ₹20,000 cr in BE 2026-27, ANRF ₹2,000 cr) should enter a time series of "money in".
- The RDI fund had zero expenditure as of 31 Jan 2026 despite a ₹20,000 crore BE, and only ₹7,131 crore of SLFM commitments by Oct 2026 — a clear implementation lag that the Standing Committee itself flagged.

### Gaps
- No public disbursement-level series for RDI (commitments vs actually invested in companies), so "money in" cannot yet be measured beyond budget lines and committed SLFM envelopes.
- No verified total for actual private co-investment mobilised by ANRF; only LoI estimates exist.
- No free source confirming how much of the India Semiconductor Mission's ₹76,000 crore envelope has actually been disbursed by FY26.
